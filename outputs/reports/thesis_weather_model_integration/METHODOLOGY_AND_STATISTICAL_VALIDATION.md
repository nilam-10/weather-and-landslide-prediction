## 1. Mathematical Formulation of the Compound Objective

Standard precipitation nowcasting pipelines treat rainfall estimation as an unweighted continuous regression problem optimized using Mean Squared Error (MSE) or Huber / Smooth-$L_1$ loss:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{MSE}} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N (\hat{q}_i - y_i)^2$$

### 1.1 The Theoretical Cause of Intensity Attenuation
Because high-intensity convective rainfall ($\ge 5.0\text{ mm/hr}$) occupies less than $5\%$ of the total spatial grid during active monsoon episodes, unconditional regression losses are heavily dominated by the vast majority of zero or light-rain pixels. Under MSE, the optimal Bayesian prediction given uncertain spatial storm advection is the **conditional expectation**:
$$\hat{q}^*(\mathbf{x}) = \mathbb{E}[Y \mid \mathbf{X} = \mathbf{x}]$$
When the exact trajectory or cell development of a convective storm has spatial uncertainty, the expected value across plausible realizations produces a diffuse, smoothed spatial blob with a dramatically lowered peak. For example, if a $30\text{ mm/hr}$ storm core could emerge in one of three adjacent locations with equal probability, the conditional expectation at each location is only $10\text{ mm/hr}$. At +180 and +360 minutes, this mathematical smoothing drives predicted rain rates below $2\text{ mm/hr}$, causing complete convective extinction.

---

### 1.2 Component 1: Intensity-Weighted L1 Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{Weighted-L1}}$)
To directly counteract conditional mean smoothing, we formulate an asymmetric, intensity-weighted regression loss that logarithmically scales penalties as observed rainfall intensity increases above the convective threshold:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{Weighted-L1}} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^N m_i \cdot w_i \cdot |\hat{q}_i - y_i|}{\sum_{i=1}^N m_i + \epsilon}$$

where:
- $\hat{q}_i \ge 0$ is the predicted continuous rain rate ($\text{mm/hr}$) from the `Softplus` activation head.
- $y_i \ge 0$ is the observed ground-truth precipitation rate ($\text{mm/hr}$) from GPM IMERG.
- $m_i \in \{0, 1\}$ is the satellite observation validity mask.
- $w_i$ is the sample-dependent intensity weight:

$$w_i = 1.0 + \alpha \cdot \log\left(1.0 + \max(0, y_i - \tau)\right)$$

#### Parameter Justification:
- **Threshold ($\tau = 5.0\text{ mm/hr}$):** Meteorologically defined threshold separating stratiform rain from convective precipitation in South Asian monsoon dynamics.
- **Scaling Factor ($\alpha = 0.5$):** Controls the gradient amplification. For $y_i < 5.0\text{ mm/hr}$, $w_i = 1.0$ (standard $L_1$). For heavy convective rain ($y_i = 30\text{ mm/hr}$), $w_i = 1.0 + 0.5 \cdot \log(26.0) \approx 2.63$, imposing more than double the gradient update on storm cells.
- **Logarithmic vs. Exponential Weighting:** Exponential weighting causes gradient explosion in the presence of extreme convective outliers ($> 60\text{ mm/hr}$). Logarithmic scaling provides stable gradient norm bounds while effectively preserving convective cores.

### 1.3 Component 2: Multiscale Differentiable Soft-FSS Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{Soft-FSS}}$)
The classical Fractions Skill Score (Roberts & Lean, 2008) is non-differentiable due to the discrete indicator step function $\mathbb{I}(\hat{q} \ge \tau)$. To optimize spatial neighborhood agreement directly through backpropagation, we formulate **Differentiable Soft-FSS**:

#### Step 1: Smooth Continuous Event Probability Field
$$\tilde{B}_{i, \text{fcst}} = \sigma\left(\frac{\hat{q}_i - \tau}{T}\right) = \frac{1}{1 + \exp\left(-\frac{\hat{q}_i - \tau}{T}\right)}$$
$$B_{i, \text{obs}} = \mathbb{I}(y_i \ge \tau)$$
where $T = 0.5\text{ mm/hr}$ is a temperature parameter governing the sharpness of the sigmoid approximation around the threshold $\tau = 5.0\text{ mm/hr}$.

#### Step 2: Spatial Neighborhood Fraction Smoothing
Spatial fractions are generated by convolving the event field with a 2D uniform pooling kernel $\mathbf{K}_k$ of radius $k$ cells:
$$\mathbf{P}_{\text{fcst}}^{(k)} = \frac{\mathbf{K}_k * (\tilde{\mathbf{B}}_{\text{fcst}} \odot \mathbf{M})}{\mathbf{K}_k * \mathbf{M} + \epsilon}$$
$$\mathbf{P}_{\text{obs}}^{(k)} = \frac{\mathbf{K}_k * (\mathbf{B}_{\text{obs}} \odot \mathbf{M})}{\mathbf{K}_k * \mathbf{M} + \epsilon}$$
where $*$ represents 2D depthwise convolution with zero-padding (mode='constant', cval=0.0). Normalizing by the convolved mask $\mathbf{K}_k * \mathbf{M}$ strictly prevents boundary distortion and handles invalid satellite pixels.

#### Step 3: Differentiable Fractions Brier Score & Soft-FSS
For each neighborhood scale $k \in K = \{1, 2, 3\}$ grid cells (corresponding to physical radii of $\approx 10\text{ km}, 20\text{ km},$ and $30\text{ km}$):
$$\text{FBS}^{(k)} = \frac{\sum_{i} m_i \cdot (P_{i, \text{fcst}}^{(k)} - P_{i, \text{obs}}^{(k)})^2}{\sum_i m_i + \epsilon}$$
$$\text{FBS}_{\text{worst}}^{(k)} = \frac{\sum_{i} m_i \cdot \left[(P_{i, \text{fcst}}^{(k)})^2 + (P_{i, \text{obs}}^{(k)})^2\right]}{\sum_i m_i + \epsilon}$$
$$\text{SoftFSS}^{(k)} = 1.0 - \frac{\text{FBS}^{(k)}}{\text{FBS}_{\text{worst}}^{(k)} + \epsilon_0}$$

The loss function minimizes the multiscale deficit:
$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{Soft-FSS}} = 1.0 - \frac{1}{|K|} \sum_{k \in K} \text{SoftFSS}^{(k)}$$

Because every operation (sigmoid, convolution, division, squaring) is continuous and differentiable, analytical gradients $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}_{\text{Soft-FSS}}}{\partial \hat{q}_i}$ flow directly back to the spatial decoder weights.

## 2. Spatial Verification Geometry: Corrected Circular Kernel

In Step 2 and Step 3 forensic audits, a major geometric inconsistency was identified in previous literature implementations: evaluating FSS using square Chebyshev windows.

### 2.1 Square vs. Circular Euclidean Neighborhoods
- **Square Chebyshev Kernel ($L = 2k + 1$):**
  Treats cells at corner offsets $(k, k)$ as identical in distance to cells at orthogonal offsets $(0, k)$. The physical distance to corners is $\sqrt{2} \cdot k \approx 1.414 \cdot k$, overestimating the effective neighborhood radius by $41.4\%$ along the diagonals.
- **Corrected Circular Euclidean Kernel:**
  Enforces true isotropic physical distance:
  $$\mathbf{K}_{\text{circular}}(u, v) = \begin{cases} 1.0 & \text{if } u^2 + v^2 \le k^2 \\ 0.0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$

### 2.2 Physical Grid Geometry in Meghalaya
The spatial grid covers $24.55^\circ\text{N} - 26.95^\circ\text{N}$ and $89.55^\circ\text{E} - 93.45^\circ\text{E}$ with $0.10^\circ$ resolution:
- **Latitude Cell Spacing:** $\Delta y = 0.10^\circ \times 110.8\text{ km/deg} = \mathbf{11.08\text{ km}}$.
- **Longitude Cell Spacing (at $25.5^\circ\text{N}$):** $\Delta x = 0.10^\circ \times 111.32 \cdot \cos(25.5^\circ) = \mathbf{10.03\text{ km}}$.
- **Average Cell Diagonal:** $\sqrt{11.08^2 + 10.03^2} = 14.95\text{ km}$.

## 3. Statistical Inferential Methodology: Stationary Block Bootstrap

### 3.1 Addressing Temporal Autocorrelation
Standard independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) resampling assumes that each forecast sequence is statistically independent. In nowcasting:
1. Forecast sequences are initialized every 30 minutes ($\Delta t = 0.5\text{ h}$).
2. Consecutive sequences share 3 input history frames ($t-90\text{m}$ to $t$).
3. Future forecast trajectories overlap across consecutive sequences (e.g., at $+360\text{m}$, twelve consecutive sequences predict overlapping time intervals).

Treating sequences as independent underestimates standard errors by a factor of $\sqrt{T_{\text{corr}}}$, resulting in artificially narrow confidence intervals and inflated false-positive significance rates.

### 3.2 Block Length Selection ($L=6$ vs. $L=12$)
To account for temporal dependence, we implemented stationary temporal block bootstrapping:
- **Block Length $L = 6$ ($3.0\text{ hours}$):**
  Appropriate for $+30\text{m}$ and $+60\text{m}$ forecast horizons where the lead time is well within the 3-hour window.
- **Block Length $L = 12$ ($6.0\text{ hours}$):**
  Required for $+360\text{m}$ (6.0 hours ahead). Using a 6-hour block ensures that sequences in adjacent resampled blocks have completely non-overlapping forecast target windows, providing true decorrelation across the entire horizon.

### 3.3 Paired Resampling Alignment
For each bootstrap replicate $b \in \{1, \dots, B\}$:
1. Contiguous blocks of sequence indices are sampled with replacement until the cohort size $N$ is reached.
2. The **exact same sample index array** `boot_idx[b]` is applied simultaneously to the precomputed sequence statistics of Exp A and Exp D.
3. The paired difference $\Delta^{[b]} = M_{\text{ExpD}}^{[b]} - M_{\text{ExpA}}^{[b]}$ is calculated for that specific replicate.
4. The 95% confidence interval is extracted via the empirical percentile method: $[P_{2.5}, P_{97.5}]$.

### 3.4 Bounded Empirical P-Value Formulation
To eliminate the uncalibrated report of `p = 0.000`, the continuity-corrected empirical $p$-value is calculated as:
$$p = \frac{k + 1}{B + 1}$$
where $k$ is the count of bootstrap replicates where $\Delta^{[b]} \le 0$ (for skill metrics where higher is better). For $B = 1,000$ iterations:
- If $k = 0$ (zero contrary replicates), the reported value is $p = \frac{1}{1001} \approx 0.000999 \implies \mathbf{p < 0.001}$.

### 4.4 Synthesis and Critical Limitations
1. **Why Exp A had artificially high displacement or skipped values at +180m:**
   Exp A predicted rainfall $\ge 5.0\text{ mm/hr}$ on only **1 sequence** out of 31 on Validation (displacement = $237.2\text{ km}$) and only **6 sequences** on Test (94.9% misses). Exp A does not achieve better placement; it simply fails to forecast storms entirely.
2. **Why Circular FSS at 30 km is $0.40\text{--}0.43$ despite ~110 km displacement:**
   The `argmax` metric evaluates only the single highest pixel in the entire $260 \times 400\text{ km}$ domain. An explicit forensic audit of all frames exhibiting displacements $> 100\text{ km}$ confirmed that **100% of these cases (9 of 9 on validation)** exhibited multiple distinct local convective peaks in both observation (4 to 22 observed peaks) and forecast (1 to 9 forecast peaks). When multiple convective clusters exist simultaneously across the plateau (e.g., western Garo Hills vs. eastern Khasi Hills, separated by $\approx 200\text{ km}$), selecting the single global peak causes an apparent coordinate offset of $150\text{--}245\text{ km}$ if the relative intensities fluctuate slightly, even though both storm clusters are actively predicted by the model.
3. **Complementary Nature of FSS and Displacement:**
   Neighborhood-based Fractions Skill Score and argmax displacement capture fundamentally different aspects of predictive quality:
   - *FSS* measures spatial frequency alignment and fractional coverage within a physical radius ($30\text{ km}$), forgiving small advective timing errors and capturing multi-cell presence.
   - *Argmax displacement* evaluates the single most intense convective core.
   Neither metric invalidates the other: FSS proves that Exp D produces actionable convective coverage within regional tolerance, while displacement diagnostics show that pinpointing the single heaviest storm cell becomes increasingly uncertain over 3 hours.
4. **Formal Limitation for Thesis:**
   This metric is a **heuristic single-peak centroid distance diagnostic** on matched frames, **NOT** an object-based multi-cell tracking algorithm (such as MODE or CRA). It must be reported as a distributional peak-offset diagnostic.
